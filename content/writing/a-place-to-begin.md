Good reading spaces make a modest promise: the words will be easy to find, and nothing else will ask for your attention. The page can have character without becoming the subject of the conversation.

## The shape of a page

A comfortable line is long enough to hold a thought and short enough to find your way back. Margins give the eye a place to rest. Headings should tell you where you are before they try to impress you.

The details do useful work, too. A link should look like a link. A quotation should feel distinct from the surrounding voice. A footnote can hold a small aside without interrupting the sentence.[^aside]

> The measure of a good page is whether someone wants to keep reading.

## Small conveniences

There is a table of contents beside longer pieces on wide screens. The slim tabs at the edges lead to the neighboring essay. The same links appear after the text, where they are easier to reach on a phone. A quiet progress line marks your place.

None of these should stand between a reader and the words. If a feature needs explaining before you can read a paragraph, it probably does not belong here.

### A note on code

Occasionally a post needs a short example. It should remain readable and be allowed to scroll on a narrow screen:

```text
Write the thought down.
Read it back.
Keep what matters.
```
